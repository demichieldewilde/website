01 · June 2026 · Journal of Mathematical Physics

Condensates shaped by harmonic functions.

For an ideal Bose gas, the geometry at the boundary can decide what condensation looks like in the bulk. We show that suitable boundary conditions for the Laplacian produce an arbitrary number of condensates in the thermodynamic limit—and that their profiles can be arbitrary harmonic functions.

The ideaBoundary conditions become a design tool for the condensate structure.

02 · March 2026 · arXiv preprint

Completing a classification of 2D SPT states.

Two-dimensional symmetry-protected topological states with a finite symmetry group were expected to be classified by H3(G, U(1)), but completeness remained open. For states prepared from a product state by a symmetric entangler, we prove that the cohomology classification is complete.

The scopeEvery state in this symmetric-entangler class is accounted for by the invariant. We can compute how many there are and how they stack.

03 · December 2025 · Communications in Mathematical Physics

How many symmetries can a spin chain hide?

We study finite-group symmetries acting on a quantum spin chain through locality-preserving automorphisms. Once symmetries that differ only by stacking with factorized actions are identified, their stable equivalence classes are completely captured by one object: the anomaly in H3(G, U(1)).

The resultThe anomaly is not merely an obstruction; it gives the complete classification.

04 · August 2025 · Astronomy & Astrophysics

Turning a patch of Sun into a whole star.

We used NESSI to turn 19 high-resolution flare observations from the Swedish 1-m Solar Telescope into synthetic full-disk spectra. The method accounts for centre-to-limb variation and differential rotation, creating a bridge between resolved solar data and the unresolved spectra of distant stars.
